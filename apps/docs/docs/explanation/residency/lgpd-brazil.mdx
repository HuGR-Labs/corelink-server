# Brazil residency & LGPD (Art. 33 §1º)

:::danger Brazil residency is not available today

**CoreLink does not offer data residency in Brazil.** Cloudflare — the substrate
CoreLink runs on — has no South-America region for R2 or D1. There is no
São Paulo bucket, and there is no way for us to create one.

An earlier version of this page stated that tenants pinned to `sam` had their
blobs, action-cache entries, audit trail and metadata *"physically located in the
South America (São Paulo) region"*, in buckets named `cas-sam`, `ac-sam` and
`audit-sam`. **That was not true and has been withdrawn.** Those buckets do not
exist; the one bucket that carries a `sam` name (`corelink-ac-sam`) is
provisioned with `locationHint=enam` — Eastern North America — and the CAS
binding for the `prod-sam` environment points at `corelink-cas-prod`, the US
bucket (`wrangler.toml`, `[[env.prod-sam.r2_buckets]]`).

Because of exactly this, `sam` is **excluded from the set of regions that can be
provisioned at signup** (`worker/src/region-map.ts`, `PROVISIONED_MACROS`), with the reason
stated in the code: its data *"would mis-land in US R2 under a false residency
label."* Signup rejects `sam` rather than accepting a tenant it cannot honour.

If your obligations to Brazilian data subjects require in-country storage,
CoreLink cannot meet them today. Please do not treat any part of this page as
an attestation that it can.

:::

This page now describes two things honestly:

1. **What CoreLink actually does with region pinning**, and which regions are
   really provisioned.
2. **What is claimed versus what is served**, so that a buyer, a DPO or a
   regulator can check us rather than trust us.

## 1. The flag, and which values are real

Every CoreLink tenant carries a `primary_region` value. The six values are a
closed enum — you cannot pass an arbitrary string. But **the enum being closed
is not the same as every value being provisioned**, and the earlier version of
this page conflated the two:

| Value | Geographic area | Provisioned today? | Where the bytes actually are |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wnam` | Western North America | **yes** | US R2 (`corelink-cas-prod`), served via `iad` |
| `enam` | Eastern North America | **yes** | US R2 (`corelink-cas-prod`), served via `iad` |
| `weur` | Western Europe | **yes** | EU R2 — CAS and AC blobs in physically-EU buckets, served via `lhr` |
| `apac` | Asia-Pacific | **yes** | `corelink-cas-apac` (Tokyo), served via `nrt`. A *location* hint for latency — R2 offers no APAC residency jurisdiction |
| `sam` | South America | **no — rejected at signup** | n/a. No Cloudflare SAM region exists |
| `afr` | Africa | **no — rejected at signup** | n/a. No provisioned colo |

The authoritative list is `PROVISIONED_MACROS` in `worker/src/region-map.ts`.
`weur` is the only value that carries a *jurisdictional* residency guarantee;
`apac` is a physical location hint, not a legal one.

## 2. What LGPD Art. 33 means for a CoreLink tenant today

CoreLink processes Brazilian personal data **outside Brazil**, in the US and the
EU. That is an international transfer under LGPD Art. 33, and it must be handled
on a transfer basis, not represented as though no transfer had occurred.

The basis CoreLink relies on is Art. 33, V (contractual necessity) together with
the standard clauses in the DPA (`legal/dpa/`), for every category of data:
workload content, action-cache entries, audit trail, tenant configuration,
billing detail, payment processing and auth tokens alike. The earlier version of
this page claimed Art. 33 *caput* — "no international transfer" — for workload
content, audit trail and tenant config. It cannot: there is no Brazilian storage
for those categories to stay in.

| Data category | Where it actually is | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Workload content (CAS) | US R2, or EU R2 for a `weur` tenant | Art. 33, V + DPA clauses |
| Action-cache entries (AC) | US R2 (`corelink-ac-*`), or EU for `weur` | Art. 33, V + DPA clauses |
| Audit trail | US R2. Append-only chain with a keyed, signed head — **tamper-evident, not immutable** | Art. 33, V + DPA clauses |
| Tenant config (D1) | Cloudflare D1 — no SAM region exists | Art. 33, V + DPA clauses |
| Telemetry (pseudonymised) | US/EU; pseudonymisation per LIA in `legal/lia/` | Art. 7 IX (legitimate interest) |
| Billing detail | Cloudflare D1 | Art. 33, V (contractual) |
| Payment processing | Stripe (global). CoreLink never sees card data | Art. 33, V + PCI-DSS handling |
| Auth tokens | Clerk (US). Email and PAT identifiers only | Art. 33, V (contractual) |

**On the audit trail specifically:** it is an append-only chain with a keyed,
signed head, so tampering is *detected at verify time*. It is **not** immutable
storage. Cloudflare R2 does not implement Object Lock — the API returns
`NotImplemented` — so a 7-year WORM retention window is not something CoreLink
can ship on this substrate, and it is not on a delivery schedule. An earlier
version of this page described a WORM window as "on the roadmap"; that was still
misleading, because the platform does not offer the primitive. And an earlier
version than that claimed we *"cannot delete an audit event even under a court
order."* We can. A lawful order to delete is technically executable.

## 3. Changing the region

Region choice happens at signup. `sam` and `afr` are rejected there.

**There is no self-service region-migration endpoint served today.** The earlier
version of this page told customers to `POST /v1/admin/tenant/region-migration`
and described a 30-day cooldown and dual Privacy-Officer + Compliance approval
"enforced at the API level, not just procedurally." That path is a design
documented in `crates/corelink-privacy/src/residency/migration.rs` — a crate
that is **not in the dependency tree of the shipped `corelink-server` binary**
(`cargo tree -p corelink-server --edges normal` does not reach it). The route is
not registered on the served router.

To change region today, contact support. The move is operational and manual.

## 4. What you can actually verify

The earlier version of this page offered four verification layers, and described
them with the wrong status code, the wrong hostname and a crate name that does
not exist. A verification instruction that fails is worse than none — it teaches
you that you checked when you did not. Here is what is actually served.

### 4.1 The cross-region guard is real, and it returns 409 — not 451

There **is** a served, fail-closed residency guard, and it works in two layers:

- The edge Worker resolves the tenant's `primary_region` from D1, strips any
  client-supplied value, fans the request out to that macro's regional Worker,
  and stamps the trusted header `x-corelink-primary-region`
  (`worker/src/index.ts`).
- The container independently re-checks that header against the colo it actually
  serves (`R2_CAS_REGION`) as a router `layer` that runs **before any handler**,
  with zero storage I/O on the reject path
  (`crates/corelink-container/src/routes/residency.rs`, wired at
  `crates/corelink-container/src/routes.rs:1063`). A mismatch — or a header
  naming a macro region that is not provisioned, such as `afr` — is rejected.

Two corrections to what the earlier text said about this:

- The rejection status is **`409 residency_violation`**, not
  `451 Unavailable for Legal Reasons` with a `legal_residency_violation` body.
  There is no `451` anywhere in `worker/src/`; the 451 mapping is defined in
  `crates/corelink-privacy/src/residency/error.rs`, in the unlinked crate of §3.
- The hostname pattern was wrong. Every production host name is flat: the `prod-sam`
  route is `sam.corelink-api.humangr.com` (`wrangler.toml`), not
  `<tenant_id>.<region>.corelink.humangr.com`.

Note also what the guard does and does not prove. It enforces that a request is
served by the container for the tenant's macro region. It cannot make a macro
region exist: for `sam` there is no in-region storage to be routed to, which is
why signup rejects `sam` outright rather than leaning on this guard.

### 4.2 Insert-level checks

The per-write pre-flight comparison of bucket region against the tenant's pinned
region, and the
`dev.hugr.corelink.residency.write_rejected_cross_region.v1` CloudEvent, belong
to the unlinked `corelink-privacy` crate. Treat them as designed, not as served.
The guard described in §4.1 is the one that runs; do not count these as a second,
independent layer in a compliance argument.

### 4.3 The property test

There is a real region-pinning property test:
`crates/corelink-privacy/tests/residency_property_region_pinning_30k.rs`. Run it
with:

```bash
cargo test -p corelink-privacy --test residency_property_region_pinning_30k
```

The earlier text named a crate that **does not exist** in this repository
(`corelink-privacy-residency-enforcement`), quoted 20,000 cases rather than the
test's own configured count, and said it "runs in CI on every commit and in the
nightly attestation job". Neither the crate nor the nightly attestation job
exists. The `cargo test -p corelink-privacy-residency-enforcement` command
printed in the earlier version could never have succeeded for anyone who tried
it.

Note the scope limit that follows from §3 and §4.1: the test exercises the
residency logic **in a crate the served binary does not link**. It is evidence
about the design, not about production.

### 4.4 The production verifier

`scripts/verify-lgpd-residency.py` exists and runs. It is **not scheduled** —
no workflow under `.github/workflows/` invokes it. The earlier text said "we run
it nightly"; we do not. It is an on-demand tool, and its results are only as
current as the last time someone ran it.

## 5. Failover

**Within-region failover:** handled by Cloudflare inside the region — D1 replica
promotion, multi-AZ R2. No cross-region action occurs.

**Regional degradation — reads DO cross regions, automatically.** This is the
most important correction on this page for anyone building a residency argument.

The earlier version said CoreLink *"does not automatically fail over to another
region"*, returns `HTTP 503 — Regional Residency Constraint`, and that a
cross-region failover is *"possible only with dual approval (Privacy Officer +
Compliance)"* — *"we hold availability hostage to residency, not the other way
round."*

What the shipped binary actually does
(`crates/corelink-container/src/routes/failover.rs`, layered in
`crates/corelink-container/src/routes.rs`): a `RollingMetricsHealthProbe`
watches this container's own live traffic (5xx rate, p99 latency, consecutive
failures over a sustained window). When the region is judged degraded:

- **Writes** are blocked fail-closed with `503 failover_readonly`, and a
  `failover.detected` audit event is emitted before the rejection. So the "503"
  half of the old claim is roughly right, for writes only, and under a different
  name.
- **Reads pass through**, stamped with
  `x-corelink-failover-read-region: <sibling>` and
  `x-corelink-failover-active: 1`, so the edge Worker — which holds the
  cross-region Service Bindings — **re-routes the read to the sibling region**.

There is no dual approval on that path, no `legal_emergency` event, and no
30-day cooldown. It is automatic, and it is a read served from a sibling
region's storage. The sibling pairs are WNAM↔ENAM and WEUR↔SAM; each APAC colo
has no sibling, so the layer is inert there.

If your transfer analysis depends on a `weur` tenant's reads never being served
from outside the EU, **this is the mechanism to assess**, and you should raise it
with us rather than rely on the withdrawn text above.

## 6. What we are not building, and what we are

Stated plainly, because LGPD Art. 6 III asks for transparency and because the
previous list mixed the two categories:

**Not available, and not scheduled — platform limits, not backlog:**

- **Brazil (`sam`) residency.** Cloudflare has no SAM region for R2 or D1.
- **R2 Object-Lock / 7-year WORM for the audit trail.** R2 returns
  `NotImplemented` for Object Lock. The append-only signed chain is what exists.

**Designed but not served — do not rely on these today:**

- The `451` residency status mapping, the insert-level cross-region write
  rejection, and the region-migration endpoint with cooldown and dual approval.
  All live in `crates/corelink-privacy`, which the shipped binary does not link.

**Genuinely available:**

- The two-layer cross-region guard of §4.1 — edge fan-out by `primary_region`
  plus the container-side `409 residency_violation` backstop.
- EU (`weur`) residency, with CAS and AC blobs in physically-EU R2 buckets.
- APAC (`apac`) as a **latency** location hint (Tokyo), not a residency
  jurisdiction.
- The append-only, signed, tamper-evident audit chain.
- Enterprise DPA amendments for telemetry suppression and for a recurring
  residency-attestation extract. Ask your account manager; legal turnaround is
  around 5 business days.

**Enterprise BYOK with customer-managed KMS** is not shipped. The activation
endpoint returns `501 byok_not_available`, and the provider integrations are not
compiled into the released binary.
