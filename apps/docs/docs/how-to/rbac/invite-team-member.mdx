# Invite a team member

## When to use this guide

You're an `Owner` or `Admin` on a CoreLink tenant and you want to add a
teammate. This guide walks through Clerk SSO setup, the invite flow, and
which role to pick.

## Prerequisites

- You are signed in as `Owner` or `Admin` on the target tenant.
- The invitee has (or can create) a Clerk-backed identity at the same SSO
  domain your tenant uses.
- You have decided which role to assign (see decision tree below).

## Pick the right role

| Their job                                                      | Role to assign   |
|----------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|
| Engineer who reads/writes the cache from CI or local dev       | `Developer`      |
| Tech lead / SRE who needs to mint tokens or read audit log     | `Admin`          |
| Co-founder / Head of Engineering who also owns billing         | `Owner`          |
| External auditor or read-only dashboard consumer               | `Viewer`         |

When in doubt, pick the **least-privileged** role that lets them do their
job. You can always promote later. Demotion is also one click.

> **A note on `Owner`:** Only assign `Owner` if the person should see the
> invoice and be able to act as the second approver for destructive
> operations. Most engineering hires should start as `Admin` or
> `Developer`.

## Step 1 — Confirm Clerk SSO is configured

CoreLink delegates authentication to Clerk. Before inviting, make sure:

1. Your Clerk application is wired to CoreLink (one-time tenant setup —
   done at signup; if you skipped this, contact support).
2. Your invitee's email domain is on your Clerk allowlist (if you use
   domain-restricted sign-up).
3. MFA is enforced for `Owner` and `Admin` roles. **The dual-approval gate
   requires MFA freshness ≤ 30 minutes** for any approver
   (`MFA_MAX_AGE_MS` in
   `crates/corelink-dual-approval/src/gate.rs`). If MFA isn't enabled for
   the user, they can sign in but they cannot approve destructive ops.

## Step 2 — Send the invite

From the tenant admin UI:

```
1. Settings → Members → "Invite member"
2. Enter the invitee's email.
3. Pick the role (defaults to Developer — see table above).
4. Click "Send invite".
```

Behind the scenes:

- A Clerk invitation email is sent.
- A pending `membership` row is **not** yet written — it's created on
  first successful sign-in. This means an unaccepted invite leaves no
  membership row to revoke later.
- An audit event is emitted: `corelink.admin.member.invited` (CloudEvent).

### Equivalent CLI command

```bash
corelink admin members invite \
  --email teammate@example.com \
  --role developer
```

## Step 3 — Invitee accepts the invite

1. They click the email link → land in Clerk → complete sign-in (or
   sign-up + MFA setup if first time).
2. Clerk redirects back to CoreLink.
3. CoreLink writes the `membership` row binding their `user_id` to your
   `tenant_id` with the role you assigned.
4. Audit event: `corelink.admin.member.joined`.

The invitee can now sign in directly to the CoreLink admin UI.

## Step 4 — Verify the role

```bash
corelink admin members list --tenant <tenant_slug>
```

Sample output:

```
USER                                  EMAIL_HASH         ROLE        SINCE
019095a3-...-3f4a                     a3f9...c821        developer   2026-05-15T14:02:00Z
019095a3-...-7c1b                     b71d...e442        admin       2026-04-12T09:30:00Z
019095a3-...-9d8e                     ee44...10c3        owner       2026-01-04T16:18:00Z
```

> Note `email_hash` — raw emails are not stored
> (`INV-AUDIT-NO-RAW-PII`). The hash is the deterministic HMAC-SHA256 of
> the canonical-form email; we use it for forensics and dedupe.

## Step 5 — Mint their first PAT (Developer only)

`Developer` users can mint their own PATs from the admin UI; `Viewer`
cannot. If you want to pre-mint a CI service-account PAT for them:

```bash
corelink admin pat mint \
  --tenant <tenant_slug> \
  --issued-to-user 019095a3-...-3f4a \
  --scopes cache:r,cache:w,cache:find-missing \
  --ttl 90d
```

The PAT is displayed **once**. Hand it to the invitee through your secret
manager (1Password, Doppler, your CI's secret store). The Argon2id hash
is what we persist; the raw PAT is irrecoverable after this step.

## Common pitfalls

1. **"They got the invite but the link is expired."** Clerk invite emails
   expire in 14 days. Re-issue from Settings → Members.
2. **"They signed in but I don't see them in the member list."** Membership
   rows are written on first successful sign-in. If they bounced off a
   Clerk error, the membership wasn't created. Check Clerk's audit log.
3. **"I want to invite a Developer but the role dropdown is grayed out."**
   You're an `Admin`, not `Owner`, and the role you tried to assign is
   above your level. Only `Owner` can grant `Owner`.
4. **"The invitee complains they can't approve a destructive op."** Check:
   (a) they're `Admin` or `Owner`; (b) MFA is enabled on their account;
   (c) their last MFA challenge was within the last 30 minutes
   (`MFA_MAX_AGE_MS`).
