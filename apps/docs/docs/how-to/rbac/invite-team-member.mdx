# Invite a team member

## When to use this guide

You're an `Owner` or `Admin` on a CoreLink tenant and you want to add a
teammate. This guide walks through Clerk SSO setup, the invite flow, and
which role to pick.

## Prerequisites

- You are signed in as `Owner` or `Admin` on the target tenant.
- The invitee has (or can create) a Clerk-backed identity at the same SSO
  domain your tenant uses.
- You have decided which role to assign (see decision tree below).

## Pick the right role

| Their job                                                      | Role to assign   |
|----------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|
| Engineer who reads/writes the cache from CI or local dev       | `Developer`      |
| Tech lead / SRE who needs to mint tokens or read audit log     | `Admin`          |
| Co-founder / Head of Engineering who also owns billing         | `Owner`          |
| External auditor or read-only dashboard consumer               | `Viewer`         |

When in doubt, pick the **least-privileged** role that lets them do their
job. You can always promote later. Demotion is also one click.

> **A note on `Owner`:** Only assign `Owner` if the person should see the
> invoice and be able to act as the second approver for destructive
> operations. Most engineering hires should start as `Admin` or
> `Developer`.

## Step 1 — Confirm Clerk SSO is configured

CoreLink delegates authentication to Clerk. Before inviting, make sure:

1. Your Clerk application is wired to CoreLink (one-time tenant setup —
   done at signup; if you skipped this, contact support).
2. Your invitee's email domain is on your Clerk allowlist (if you use
   domain-restricted sign-up).
3. MFA is enforced for `Owner` and `Admin` roles. **The dual-approval gate
   requires MFA freshness ≤ 30 minutes** for any approver
   (`MFA_MAX_AGE_MS` in
   `crates/corelink-dual-approval/src/gate.rs`). If MFA isn't enabled for
   the user, they can sign in but they cannot approve destructive ops.

## Step 2 — Send the invite

Team management lives in the dashboard, at
[https://humangr.com/corelink/customer/team](https://humangr.com/corelink/customer/team):

1. Open the **Team** page.
2. Under **Invite a teammate**, enter the invitee's email address.
3. Pick the **Role** (see the table above).
4. Click **Send invite**.

Behind the scenes:

- The seat is recorded immediately as a `team_member` row with status
  `invited` — that is what the **Pending invitations** list shows.
- Only a pseudonymised hash of the trimmed, lower-cased email is stored;
  the raw address is never persisted.
- A customer-visible audit row with event type `team.invited` is written
  *before* the seat row, so an invite can never commit without its audit
  entry. Its detail names the role only, never the address.

### Automating it over the HTTP API

There is **no `corelink` CLI command for team management** — the two
supported paths are the dashboard above and the HTTP API below. The API
accepts either a CoreLink PAT or your signed-in Clerk session, so you can
script invites from CI with a PAT:

```bash
curl -X POST https://corelink-api.humangr.com/v1/customer/team/invite \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $CORELINK_PAT" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"email":"teammate@example.com","role":"Developer"}'
```

Inviting (and removing) a member is **owner/admin only** — a `Developer`
or `Viewer` token gets a `403` here, whichever path it uses.

## Step 3 — Invitee accepts the invite

1. The invitee signs in to CoreLink — or signs up and sets up MFA, if
   it's their first time — using **the same email address you invited**.
2. Clerk redirects back to CoreLink.
3. CoreLink matches the pending seat by its email hash, rebinds it to
   their real `user_id`, and flips its status from `invited` to `active`.
   The role you chose at invite time is preserved.

They now appear under **Members** instead of **Pending invitations**.

## Step 4 — Verify the role

On the **Team** page, the **Members** table lists everyone with access and
the role each one holds; people who have been invited but have not signed
in yet appear under **Pending invitations**.

To check the same thing from a script:

```bash
curl https://corelink-api.humangr.com/v1/customer/team \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $CORELINK_PAT"
```

The response carries a `members` array; each entry has the member's
`user_id`, `role`, `joined_at` and `status` (`invited` until they sign in,
then `active`). An `email` field is present in the shape, but it comes back
as a placeholder — see the note below.

> **CoreLink never stores the raw invitee email.** The invite persists
> only a pseudonymised hash of the trimmed, lower-cased address
> (`CTRL-PRIV-001`), and that hash is also the key used to match the
> invitation when the person signs in. Audit rows follow the same rule
> (`INV-AUDIT-NO-RAW-PII`): the `team.invited` entry records the role, not
> the address.

## Step 5 — Mint their first PAT (Developer and above)

`Developer`, `Admin` and `Owner` users can mint their own PATs from the
**API keys** page of the dashboard
(`https://humangr.com/corelink/customer/keys`); `Viewer` cannot.

If you'd rather pre-mint a CI service-account token yourself, use the keys
API — again, there is no CLI for this:

```bash
curl -X POST https://corelink-api.humangr.com/v1/customer/keys \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $CORELINK_PAT" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"name":"ci-service-account","scopes":["cache:r","cache:w","cache:find-missing"]}'
```

The request body is only a name and a list of scopes — there is no field
for issuing a token *on another user's behalf*, so a service-account token
belongs to the tenant, not to the invitee. See the
[Permissions reference](/reference/rbac/permissions) for every scope you
can grant.

The token secret is displayed **once**. Hand it to the invitee through
your secret manager (1Password, Doppler, your CI's secret store); it is
irrecoverable after this step. A token can be revoked at any time with
`POST /v1/customer/keys/{pat_id}/revoke`.

## Common pitfalls

1. **"They signed in but they're still under Pending invitations."** A
   seat only flips to `active` when the address Clerk reports matches the
   address you invited, after normalisation (trimmed and lower-cased). A
   sign-in with a different address — an alias, a personal account —
   leaves the invited seat untouched. Re-invite the address they actually
   sign in with.
2. **"My invite came back 403."** Three gates run, in this order: an
   invite carrying the `Owner` role is always rejected (a tenant has
   exactly one owner, its creator); the caller must itself be `Owner` or
   `Admin`; and only the `Owner` may invite a privileged role (`Admin`).
3. **"The invitee complains they can't approve a destructive op."** Check:
   (a) they're `Admin` or `Owner`; (b) MFA is enabled on their account;
   (c) their last MFA challenge was within the last 30 minutes
   (`MFA_MAX_AGE_MS`).
