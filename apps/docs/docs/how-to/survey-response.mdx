# Responding to a CoreLink customer-health survey

You're here because you clicked a link from a CoreLink customer-health
survey email (NPS, CSAT, free-text, or multi-choice). This page
explains how your response is handled, what we store, and what we
don't.

## What we store

When you submit a response, we record exactly four things:

1. **Your numeric score or text answer** — the actual content of your
   response, kept verbatim.
2. **A salted hash of your email address** (`recipient_hash`) — a
   one-way fingerprint we use to deduplicate replies and to tie a
   response to a tenant for trend analysis. **We do not store your
   raw email** in the survey table.
3. **The tenant ID** the survey was issued against — this is your
   organization's CoreLink workspace identifier.
4. **A salted hash of your IP address and user-agent string** — used
   only for abuse detection (see [our abuse
   runbook](https://github.com/HumanGuardrail/corelink-server/blob/main/specs/_runbooks/RB-SURVEY-ABUSE.md))
   and never displayed in any analyst dashboard.

We do **not** store your raw IP address, your raw user-agent string,
or your email body or name in this table.

## How your response is used

Your response is **anonymous in aggregate but tied to your tenant_id
for trend analysis.** Concretely:

- The CS team reads survey data through weekly **aggregates** (NPS
  per tier, CSAT per surface, free-text top themes) — see the [CS
  team's analysis protocol](https://github.com/HumanGuardrail/corelink-server/blob/main/marketing/retention/customer-health/SURVEY-ANALYSIS-PROTOCOL.md).
- Lighthouse-customer accounts (those who agreed to deeper engagement
  via the [lighthouse program](https://github.com/HumanGuardrail/corelink-server/blob/main/marketing/lighthouse-kit/CUSTOMER-PLAYBOOK.md))
  get a **per-tenant pulse view** so the CS lead can spot drift
  inside an individual customer.
- **Your individual score is never tied back to your name or email
  on any internal dashboard.** The CS team sees `recipient_hash` (a
  64-char hex digest), not the raw email.

## Token security

The link in your invite email carries an HMAC-SHA256 signature over a
payload that includes your `recipient_hash`, the survey ID, and an
expiry timestamp. We verify the signature in constant time before
recording your response. This means:

- The link **authenticates you without a password** — the HMAC stands
  in for credentials.
- The link **expires** (typically after 7-14 days from issuance —
  the exact TTL is set by the wave; see
  [NPS-SURVEY-SCHEDULE](https://github.com/HumanGuardrail/corelink-server/blob/main/marketing/retention/customer-health/NPS-SURVEY-SCHEDULE.md)).
- The link **works once.** A second submission on the same link is
  rejected (replay protection).
- If someone forwards your link, they can submit a response **once**
  on your behalf, but they cannot reuse it after that. If you
  suspect your email was compromised, please contact
  [security@hugr.dev](mailto:security@hugr.dev).

## Deleting your response

If you want a response deleted, use the standard CoreLink Data
Subject Request (DSR) flow — see the
[Privacy Center](/trust/privacy-center) → "Erasure request". The
survey response table is included in the canonical 12-backend erasure
worker (`corelink-privacy-erasure-worker`); your `recipient_hash` is
the join key.

## Audit trail

Every successful and rejected survey response is recorded in the
CoreLink audit chain
(`corelink-audit`). The CS team's read access to survey data is
itself audited via the same outbox — so we have a tamper-evident
record of who looked at what, when.
