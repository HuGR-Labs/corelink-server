# CoreLink vs EngFlow

EngFlow is the Bazel ecosystem authority. The company was founded by
Ulf Adams (former Bazel tech lead at Google) and Helen Altshuler (ex
Google Bazel onboarding lead), raised an $18M Series A from a16z /
Tiger Global / firstminute in 2022, and speaks at every BazelCon. If
your purchasing criterion is "the people who built Bazel should sell
us the Bazel platform", EngFlow is the literal answer.

CoreLink is a different shape. We are a polyglot **cache-only** product
with self-serve pricing, BYOK across four KMS providers, and an
RFC 6962 audit chain. We do not try to out-Bazel the Bazel creators
on Bazel-purist ground; we sell against EngFlow on **pricing
transparency**, **polyglot reach**, and **time-to-decision** for the
mid-market platform engineer.

This page lays out the honest tradeoffs. Dated **2026-05-27**; verify
the EngFlow pricing page before quoting in a buying decision —
EngFlow's pricing is contact-gated on both tiers and changes by deal.

## TL;DR

- **Pick EngFlow if** you are a Bazel monolith shop (500+ engineers,
  RBE-heavy, dedicated build infra team) and you want the Bazel
  creators on your support escalation path. Be ready for a contact-sales
  workflow on both the Free and Enterprise tiers.
- **Pick CoreLink if** you want a published price on the page, REAPI
  cache that works with Bazel **and** Cargo / npm / pip / OCI, BYOK on
  four KMS providers as a default feature, and a 3–14 day trial-to-buy
  cycle that fits inside a platform engineer's unsigned-authority
  ceiling.
- **Either is fine if** Bazel-only is your world and you have
  procurement bandwidth for a multi-week sales cycle.

## Where EngFlow wins

We say this without irony: EngFlow is excellent at what it does.

1. **Deepest Bazel + adjacent-tool coverage in the market.** Beyond
   Bazel, EngFlow supports Goma (Chromium-style distributed compile),
   Soong (Android Open Source Project build system), Reclient,
   BuildStream, Pants, Please, and `recc`. If your stack is Chromium,
   AOSP, or any of the long-tail REAPI-adjacent build systems with
   non-trivial integration debt, EngFlow has probably already done the
   integration work. CoreLink supports the REAPI v2 contract — that
   covers Bazel, Buck2, Pants, Please, BuildStream, recc — but we do
   not advertise Goma or Soong integration depth.

2. **Remote execution as a first-class product.** EngFlow runs Linux,
   macOS, **and** Windows executor fleets. The autoscaler is a real
   product. The "we will run your Bazel build farm so your SREs do not
   have to" pitch is genuinely the differentiator. CoreLink does not
   ship RBE; if your buying criterion is "make the EKS Bazel cluster
   go away", EngFlow (or BuildBuddy) is the conversation.

3. **The founders' provenance.** Ulf Adams was the Bazel tech lead at
   Google. Helen Altshuler led Bazel onboarding at Google. When the
   Bazel community asks "who do we trust to run remote execution at
   scale", the answer is recursive — the people who designed remote
   execution. CoreLink has no such pedigree to draw on. For a
   1,000-engineer monorepo where the cost of getting Bazel wrong is
   measured in weeks of lost productivity, that provenance has real
   buying weight.

4. **Self-host with both flavors.** EngFlow's public materials
   advertise both SaaS managed and self-host (run on your AWS / GCP /
   Azure account, your network, your IAM). For an enterprise with a
   "no third-party data plane" mandate, this is a clean answer.
   CoreLink's data plane is Cloudflare-edge today; if your threat model
   excludes Cloudflare, we are out of contention.

5. **Funded.** $18M Series A from name-brand VCs. A 1,000-engineer
   procurement department reads this as "the vendor will exist in 5
   years." A solo-founder CoreLink reads, to that same procurement
   department, as "vendor-risk score = high." That is a real win for
   EngFlow at the enterprise tier.

## Where CoreLink wins

Honest differentiation, not shade.

1. **Self-serve pricing, with a number on the page.** EngFlow gates
   both the Free tier and the Enterprise tier on a contact form. There
   is no published mid-tier. A platform engineer who wants to evaluate
   the cache **today** cannot — they have to book a sales call. CoreLink
   posts $25/mo Pro on the page. Free signup needs no credit card.
   First cache hit in minutes. This is the difference between
   *evaluating a cache* and *evaluating a vendor relationship*.

2. **Buyer-authority fit for the mid-market.** Per our ICP audit
   (Persona P1 — Platform Lead at compliance-aware Series B/C SaaS, 50–
   250 eng), the unsigned-authority budget is typically $500–$2,000/mo.
   CoreLink lands inside that window with a card and a click; EngFlow's
   sales-led shape lands outside it by definition. If your buyer is the
   VP-Eng at a 1,000-engineer org, EngFlow's friction is fine — they
   are paid to run procurement. If your buyer is the platform lead at a
   150-engineer org, EngFlow's friction is the bug.

3. **Polyglot, not Bazel-ecosystem-only.** Cargo, npm, pip, brew, OCI
   artifacts on the same content-addressable store. One bill, one
   audit log, one quota, one BYOK envelope. Today, a polyglot org
   stitches EngFlow (Bazel) + sccache + S3 (Rust / C++) + Turborepo
   (JS / TS) + a private registry (OCI) + four security reviews.
   CoreLink collapses that into one workspace.

4. **BYOK by default, on four KMS providers.** AWS KMS, GCP KMS,
   Azure Key Vault, HashiCorp Vault. Customer-held KEK; CoreLink
   decrypts a Data Encryption Key into memory for at most 5 minutes;
   revocation produces an Ed25519-signed erasure attestation. EngFlow's
   public page does not advertise a 4-KMS BYOK story at the cache
   tier. (EngFlow's self-host option lets you BYO infrastructure end-
   to-end, which is a different — and stronger — privacy posture in
   one direction; the tradeoff is the operational cost of self-host.)

5. **RFC 6962 / RFC 3161 audit chain, re-derivable.** Your auditor
   downloads the log shard, replays it, and recomputes the published
   Merkle root. Same trust model as Certificate Transparency. This is
   engineered for the SOC 2 Type II auditor's first interview.
   EngFlow does not advertise a comparable audit chain.

6. **TLA+-verified tenant isolation invariant.** The cross-tenant
   safety property is model-checked in CI; counterexamples block merge
   to `main`. The model is public. This is the kind of artifact that
   matters to engineering-trust buyers who actually open the spec
   folder.

7. **No remote execution to operate or be billed for.** If you are
   happy with GitHub Actions runners, Buildkite agents, or your own
   Kubernetes job runners, CoreLink charges you $0 for the executor
   you are not using. EngFlow's pricing structure assumes RBE is part
   of the deal.

## Cost comparison: a concrete scenario

We cannot give you EngFlow's number — EngFlow does not post it. What
we can do is give you a like-for-like CoreLink number and an
honest sketch of what an EngFlow deal looks like at the same scale.

**Scenario.** 100-engineer Series C fintech. ~50 monorepo builds/day,
~200 GB warm cache, ~12M cache requests/month, mostly Linux CI with a
small macOS fleet for the iOS client. SOC 2 Type II audit on the
calendar in 90 days. Bazel + sccache (Rust) + Turborepo (JS frontend).

### CoreLink

- **Pro: $25/mo flat.** 500 GB included (you use 200), 20M requests
  included (you use 12M). One bill covers Bazel CAS + Cargo cache +
  Turborepo adapter.
- BYOK + audit-chain are at the Enterprise tier; for the SOC 2 push,
  open an Enterprise conversation when the auditor signals
  customer-managed keys are required. Published anchor range:
  $500–$5,000/mo depending on storage + dedicated infra + BYOK + SSO +
  SLA.
- Self-serve trial-to-decision: typically 3–14 days. No procurement
  cycle below $2k/mo.

### EngFlow

- Public pricing page: contact-gated. No number posted. We will not
  invent one.
- Anecdotal range from public industry conversations (not from
  EngFlow): enterprise deals for the Bazel + RBE bundle land in
  $40k–$500k/yr depending on executor hours, RBE fleet size, and
  support tier. EngFlow targets P2 (VP-Eng at 500+ eng monorepo)
  rather than P1 (Platform Lead at 50–250 eng).
- Sales cycle: weeks to months. Includes a PoC, security
  questionnaire, DPA + MSA negotiation, internal champion recruitment.
- The Bazel + macOS RBE story is real value at scale. For a 100-
  engineer fintech that does not yet run a Bazel RBE cluster, that
  value is mostly aspirational — you are paying for capacity you do
  not have a roadmap to use.

### What the bill **does not** show

- **Time-to-decision cost.** A 90-day SOC 2 deadline is incompatible
  with a 90-day enterprise sales cycle. The CoreLink Pro tier lets you
  start the work this week and convert to Enterprise when the auditor
  requires it. With EngFlow, the sales cycle **is** the timeline.
- **Polyglot stitching tax.** EngFlow handles the Bazel side; the
  Rust and JS sides still need sccache + Turborepo + their own audit
  posture. That is three vendors and three security reviews instead of
  one.
- **Procurement friction.** Two contracts, two DPAs, two annual
  renewals, two pricing renegotiations vs one — at 100 engineers, that
  is real overhead the bill never shows.

## Migration considerations

You are on EngFlow and curious about CoreLink. Concrete steps:

1. **Provision a CoreLink workspace** at
   `https://corelink-app.humangr.com/sign-up`. No credit card, no sales
   call. You will land on the Free tier (10 GB cache, 500k req/mo).
2. **Add CoreLink as a shadow cache.** Bazel supports a chain of
   caches via `--remote_cache` + `--experimental_remote_downloader`.
   Run EngFlow as primary and CoreLink as shadow for one week. Measure
   cache-hit ratio, p95 GET latency, p95 PUT latency, error rate. The
   numbers should be within noise — both are content-addressable
   stores reading the same hashes.
3. **Decide which axis to cut over.** EngFlow's RBE has no CoreLink
   equivalent. Two viable patterns:
   - **Cache-on-CoreLink, RBE-on-EngFlow.** Keep EngFlow for executor
     pool; move `--remote_cache` to CoreLink. Useful if RBE is a
     genuine workload (you have macOS / Windows executor hours that
     justify it).
   - **Cache-on-CoreLink, no RBE.** Drop the EngFlow contract; rely on
     GitHub Actions / Buildkite for CI compute. The right move if
     EngFlow RBE is shelfware or if your build matrix is mostly
     Linux-on-Linux without scaling problems.
4. **Add Cargo / npm / OCI workloads.** Once Bazel is cut over, point
   `sccache` (or your equivalent), the Turborepo remote cache, and
   your OCI tag pulls at CoreLink. One bill, one audit log, one BYOK
   envelope.
5. **BYOK rotation when the SOC 2 auditor asks.** On Enterprise, point
   CoreLink at your KMS (AWS / GCP / Azure / Vault). Rotation is
   operator-controlled.

You do not have to leave EngFlow to take CoreLink cache. If EngFlow's
RBE is doing real work, leave it doing real work. CoreLink is happy to
be your cache and Cargo and JS partner.

## When NOT to choose CoreLink

We lose three buying conversations cleanly to EngFlow.

1. **You are a Bazel monolith at 500+ engineers and you need RBE.**
   This is EngFlow's home segment and they earn it. Mac and Windows
   executor pools, autoscaling, the Bazel creators on the support
   escalation path — there is no part of that we can match in 2026.
   CoreLink does not ship RBE.

2. **You require a self-hosted-on-your-own-infrastructure data
   plane.** EngFlow advertises self-host. CoreLink runs on Cloudflare
   edge with BYOK; we do not deploy into your AWS / GCP / Azure
   account today. If your security or regulatory model requires the
   data plane inside your VPC, EngFlow is the answer.

3. **Your buyer is a VP-Eng with $500k/yr procurement authority and
   a 6-month evaluation timeline.** EngFlow's sales-led shape is the
   right shape for that buyer. CoreLink's self-serve shape is the
   wrong shape for that buyer — they will read it as "small vendor,
   high risk". We are better as a tool the platform team adopts from
   below than a procurement-led top-down deal.

4. **You depend on Goma, Soong, or non-REAPI build-system
   integrations.** We do not have integration depth there. EngFlow
   does.

5. **You need a logo wall.** EngFlow has one. We are at the case-
   study-of-one stage. For a procurement-led buyer who weights social
   proof heavily, this is a real and rational gap.

We will not pretend these gaps do not exist. They do. And EngFlow is a
good company.
