Show HN: Build cache that costs $30/mo where Depot charges $200 — the math is R2 zero-egress

I'm a solo founder. I've been building CoreLink for about 6 months. It's a
multi-tenant content-addressable cache (CAS + Action Cache, REAPI v2) built
on Cloudflare's edge stack — Workers, Durable Objects, Containers, R2, D1.
Supports Bazel, Buck2, Turborepo v8, and sccache out of the box. This is not
GA (see the honest section below), but the data plane is wired, deployed, and
working against real R2 in production.

Before the pitch: here's the arithmetic I kept staring at when I started this.

**The egress math — why incumbents have a margin problem**

Take a real workload: 10-dev team, 100 GB of warm cache, 500 GB/month of reads
(reasonable for a monorepo with a CI fleet). Call it "Team Workload A."

| Provider | Storage | Egress | Compute/state | ~Monthly total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depot (Startup tier) | bundled | bundled | bundled | ~$200/mo |
| JFrog Artifactory Cloud (Pro) | included | $0.08/GB | included | ~$240/mo+ |
| Self-hosted on AWS (S3 + Bazel remote-cache) | $2.30/mo | **$45/mo** | EC2 t3.medium ~$30 | ~$80-120/mo + ops time |
| Backblaze B2 (raw, no cache logic) | $1.50/mo | $0/mo (CAP) | you build it | N/A (no product) |
| **CoreLink Team** | bundled | **$0 egress** | bundled | **$30/mo** |

The R2 egress line is not a promo. Cloudflare's R2 has no egress fees, full
stop. The S3/GCS/Azure equivalent for 500 GB/month is ~$45-50. That single
structural difference is why CoreLink can target 80-90% gross margin at $30
where a competitor on AWS infrastructure would need ~$150-200 just to stay in
the black at the same margin.

I am not saying CoreLink is better engineered than Depot. I'm saying the
infrastructure cost base is structurally different, and I want to show you
the math before I show you the product.

**What CoreLink is, precisely**

It is NOT "just a build cache." Bazel REAPI v2 and Turborepo v8 are two
protocol bridges on top of a CAS+AC primitive that also ships:

- HMAC-keyed tenant isolation (TLA+ invariant `INV-TenantIsolation`, verified
  in CI — counterexamples block merge)
- BYOK envelope encryption across 4 KMS providers (AWS KMS, GCP KMS,
  Azure Key Vault, HashiCorp Vault). Customer-held KEK; 5-minute in-memory DEK
  cap; disable the key and we cannot read your data
- RFC 6962 Merkle-chained audit log: BLAKE3 leaves, Ed25519-signed hourly roots,
  RFC 3161 timestamps. Append-only. Exportable as NDJSON with re-verifiable
  inclusion proofs offline — no trust in CoreLink required
- GDPR/LGPD DSR execution, dual-approval workflows, DPA enforcement
- Argon2id PAT auth, FastCDC content-defined chunking, multi-region replication
  coordinator (4 regions: `wnam`, `enam`, `weur`, `sam`)
- 47 TLA+ specs, 264 proptest blocks across 87 Cargo packages

87 Rust crates. It is a governance platform that happens to have a build cache
on top. The target customer is the team that wants Vercel-grade onboarding AND
a controls story they can hand to an auditor — without paying enterprise prices.

**What's open / what's closed**

13 crates are dual MIT/Apache-2.0 and going to crates.io: the CLI, schema
crates, BLAKE3 verifier primitives, FFI wrappers, and the Bazel/Turbo bridge
adapters. The server-side platform (CAS engine, auth, billing, audit chain,
BYOK) stays closed. The per-crate boundary is documented in
[`docs/internal/OSS-VS-CLOSED-MATRIX.md`](../../docs/internal/OSS-VS-CLOSED-MATRIX.md).
The OSS strategy is: seed attention + let people audit the wire protocol without
handing away the ops value.

**What is NOT done — the honest list**

I'll be upfront because I'd rather have people who read the tradeoffs as
customers than people who are surprised later:

1. **Not GA.** Production infra is live (CAS + AC persist to real R2, PAT auth
   + tenant isolation work E2E, 5 endpoints live at `corelink.humangr.com`).
   What's missing before I call it GA: external pentest, SOC 2 Type I,
   30-day staging burn-in, and 3 lighthouse customers. That's calendar work,
   not code debt.

2. **Only one region exercised live.** The `wnam` (IAD) region is the only one
   with real traffic. Multi-region replication is implemented and tested
   in staging; other regions have not received real customer load yet.

3. **Stripe billing not yet self-serve E2E.** Stripe is integrated. The
   upgrade/downgrade flow and metered billing are coded. The self-serve checkout
   UI is a stub — I'm still wiring the final happy path. Right now I provision
   new tenants manually via the admin CLI.

4. **Admin UI is MVP-only.** Functional for tenant management and audit log
   browsing. Not where I want the DX to be. It's not a replacement for a
   dashboard you'd show a VP of Engineering.

5. **Bazel ergonomics need community feedback.** The REAPI v2 bridge works.
   I haven't run it against a large monorepo with 1000+ targets in production.
   I want to hear from people who actually use Bazel at scale about friction
   points.

6. **Solo founder.** I am the only person on this. Response time on issues
   will be honest, not SLA-padded. I will answer every technical question
   here personally.

## Meta: title alternatives with rationale

### Option A (recommended)
**Show HN: Build cache that costs $30/mo where Depot charges $200 — the math is R2 zero-egress**

Rationale: Leads with a concrete, falsifiable claim (price comparison) and
immediately provides the mechanism (R2 zero-egress). Invites skepticism, which
is the correct HN first-click hook. The specific competitor name (Depot) makes
it tangible — readers who pay $200/mo will click. Avoids the cardinal sin of
"disrupting" language; it's an arithmetic statement. 68 characters — fits HN's
80-char soft limit comfortably.

### Option B
**Show HN: Multi-tenant Bazel remote cache in Rust — BLAKE3 + TLA+ tenant isolation + R2 zero-egress**

Rationale: Technical-depth angle for the subset of HN that cares about TLA+
and formal verification. Likely to surface in comments ("TLA+ for a cache?
tell me more") which drives long threads. Weaker click-through than Option A
because it front-loads stack rather than benefit. Use this if posting on a day
when "Systems / PL" topics are dominating the front page.

### Option C
**Show HN: I built a content-addressable cache SaaS solo in 6 months — R2 made the unit economics work**

Rationale: Solo-founder narrative hook. HN readers are historically sympathetic
to genuine "I built this alone" posts, especially when the technical depth
backs it up. Positions the post as an engineering-story thread rather than a
product pitch, which reduces the "this is marketing" flag. Weaker if the
algorithm is skewing toward established projects that week. Good fallback if
Options A or B land without traction in the first hour — a repost under this
angle gets a different audience.

## Posting strategy

**Recommended day/time:** Tuesday or Wednesday, 9–11 am Eastern Time.

Research on HN front-page mechanics consistently shows weekday late-morning
ET is the highest-traffic window with the best combination of:
- Active voters online (US East Coast + Europe overlap)
- Moderators present (flagged posts get reviewed faster = less risk of
  silent demotion)
- Competition from weekend megathreads dissipated by Monday

Avoid Monday (weekend backlog dominates), Friday (engagement drops off by
midday), and any day a major product/tech announcement is expected to absorb
attention.

**In the thread:** Reply to every technical comment within the first 2 hours.
Early engagement velocity is correlated with front-page persistence. The
honest-tradeoffs section of the post is designed to pre-empt "but is it GA?"
skepticism — acknowledge it, don't deflect.

**Coordinate with:** A Twitter/X post at the same time pointing to the HN
thread (not to the product directly) amplifies voting surface without
triggering HN's "shill" detector.
